Whereupon the assembly, being greatly troubled, did adjourn unto the morrow, that counsel might be taken in quieter hours. Divers among them misliked the proposal, yet none durst speak against it openly, fearing the displeasure of the elder men.

In those days the road beyond the ford was little travelled, and such as ventured thither went armed. It was said that robbers kept the wood, though none had seen them these many seasons. The tale nevertheless sufficed to keep honest folk at home.

The steward, a man both prudent and exceeding cautious, made inventory of the stores. He found the granary wanting, the salt near spent, and the casks but half filled. These tidings he conveyed with much reluctance.

At cockcrow they set forth, the frost lying hard upon the fields. Their progress was slow, for the beasts were ill fed and the way uneven. Yet none complained, knowing what delay would cost them.

Coming at length to the river, they beheld the bridge broken. Some counselled to wait, others to ford the stream, and thus the day was well nigh spent in argument. The elder prevailed, and they crossed at the shallows.

Of that winter little is written, save that it was long and the dearth grievous. When spring came the fields were sown late, and the harvest after was but meagre. Such was the manner of those years.
